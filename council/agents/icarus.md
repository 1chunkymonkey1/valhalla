THE STATE OF JEFFERSON
CONTEXT BRIEF FOR NEW READERS

WHAT IS JEFFERSON?
The State of Jefferson is a proposed new US state encompassing the rural northern third of California and the southern third of Oregon. The movement has existed since 1941, when residents of Siskiyou County, CA and Josephine County, OR declared themselves the State of Jefferson — one week before Pearl Harbor ended the effort. It has never died. It has never succeeded. Until now.
Jefferson is not a secession movement. It is a sovereignty movement. The distinction is everything. We are not leaving California. We are building something better here — and asking for the recognition we have already earned.

THE TERRITORY
Total population: Approximately 2.5 million people
Geographic scope: Northern third of California + southern third of Oregon
California counties: Siskiyou, Modoc, Trinity, Shasta, Lassen, Tehama, Humboldt, Del Norte, Mendocino, Lake, Glenn, Colusa, Butte, Plumas, Sierra, Nevada, El Dorado, Amador, Calaveras, Tuolumne, Mariposa, Alpine
Oregon counties: Josephine, Jackson, Klamath, Lake, Curry, Douglas, Coos
Key city: Sonora, CA — Tuolumne County seat. Heart of the movement.

THE WATER NUMBERS — THE CORE ARGUMENT
This is the single most important fact in the Jefferson argument:
Approximately 75% of California's freshwater originates in Jefferson territory. Approximately 75% of California's demand for that water comes from everywhere else.
Jefferson territory contains the headwaters of the Sacramento River, Feather River, Tuolumne River, Trinity River, Klamath River, and Eel River — the primary freshwater sources for the entire state.
State Water Project (SWP) — Key Facts:
	•	Built to deliver approximately 4.2 million acre-feet of water per year from northern California
	•	Delivers an average of 2.4 million acre-feet annually — spanning 700+ miles of canals, tunnels, and pipelines
	•	Serves 27 million Californians — more than half the state's population
	•	66% of SWP water goes to homes and businesses, primarily in Southern California and the Bay Area
	•	The Tuolumne River watershed — Eason Greene's home watershed — feeds the Hetch Hetchy reservoir supplying San Francisco
	•	The SWP's primary source is the Feather River — entirely within Jefferson territory

THE NEW YORK / CATSKILLS COMPARISON
New York City faced the same problem in the 1990s: a rural watershed providing water to millions of urban residents, with no compensation mechanism for the communities doing the stewardship.
Their solution became the most celebrated ecosystem services model in American history:
	•	NYC invested $1–1.5 billion in the Catskill watershed communities through land acquisition and farm stewardship payments
	•	This avoided a $6–8 billion water filtration plant — saving billions while compensating rural communities
	•	The Watershed Agricultural Council now works with farm and forest landowners to protect water quality on behalf of 9 million New York residents
	•	The model is now internationally recognized as a successful public-private partnership
Jefferson's ask is identical: fair compensation for the water stewardship Jefferson communities provide to Los Angeles and San Francisco. We are not asking to keep the water. We are asking to be paid what it is worth.

THE INFRASTRUCTURE GAP
Jefferson counties provide California's most critical natural resource — water — and receive among the state's worst infrastructure investment in return:
	•	Rural residents travel more than twice the distance for healthcare compared to urban counterparts — 17.8 miles vs 8.1 miles on average
	•	Emergency medical services take twice as long to arrive in rural areas as in cities — approximately 10% of rural residents wait nearly 30 minutes for emergency assistance
	•	Over 130 rural hospitals have closed nationally since 2010 — Jefferson counties are disproportionately affected
	•	Rural broadband gap: 26 million Americans still lack basic broadband access — concentrated in rural counties like those in Jefferson territory
	•	California ranks 18th nationally for broadband coverage overall — but the state average masks severe rural deficits in northern counties

THE POLITICAL REPRESENTATION GAP
Jefferson territory contains approximately 2.5 million people — roughly 6% of California's population. Yet the political reality is stark:
	•	California's 58 counties each have one seat on the County Board of Supervisors — urban counties with millions of residents have the same county-level structure as Jefferson counties with tens of thousands
	•	The California State Senate has 40 districts — Jefferson territory is represented by a handful of senators who must balance rural and urban constituents
	•	Sacramento's supermajority is coastal and urban — Jefferson counties have never had effective veto power over legislation affecting their land, water, and resources
	•	The State Water Project was designed, built, and is operated by Sacramento — Jefferson communities that host its infrastructure had no meaningful consent in its construction

THE CONSTITUTIONAL PATH TO STATEHOOD
Article IV, Section 3 of the US Constitution: new states may be admitted by Congress with consent of the relevant state legislatures.
Historical precedents for state partition: Maine from Massachusetts (1820), West Virginia from Virginia (1863), Tennessee from North Carolina territory. State partition is not unprecedented — it is constitutional.
The Alaska / Hawaii model: admitted together in 1959 — one Republican-leaning, one Democratic-leaning. Jefferson and DC statehood as a paired admission is the congressional deal structure that makes this viable for both parties.
